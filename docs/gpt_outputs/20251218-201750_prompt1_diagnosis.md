### What is the treatment vs baselines?

* **Treatment = MANOVA/FJS de-aliasing overlay**:

  1. Compute MANOVA-ish component estimators from a balanced (one-way or nested) design.
  2. Detect “outlier eigenpairs” above MP bulk edge for certain linear combinations (B(a)).
  3. Use the FJS (t)-vector logic to **attribute** an outlier to a target component and recover de-aliased spike (\hat\mu\approx \hat\lambda/t_r(\hat\lambda,a)).
  4. Overlay into a covariance object used for portfolio evaluation (either modify eigenvalues/eigenvectors of (\Sigma_{\text{between}}) before reconstruction, or blend with an external baseline estimator).
     Source: `project_state/MODULE_SUMMARIES.md#srcfjs`, `project_state/FUNCTION_INDEX.md` (`fjs/dealias.py::dealias_search`, `fjs/overlay.py::apply_overlay`, `fjs/gating.py::gate_*`).
* **Baselines**:

  * Shrinkage: Ledoit–Wolf / OAS / constant-correlation,
  * Factor: observed factor covariance; POET-lite,
  * Robust scatter: Tyler and Huber variants and their MP-edge computations,
  * Possibly RIE/QuEST-based variants (names implied by reporting and module structure).
    Source: `project_state/MODULE_SUMMARIES.md#srcfinance`, `project_state/MODULE_SUMMARIES.md#srcevaluation`.

## Theory ↔ implementation alignment

### Minimal theory assumptions (what the FJS paper actually needs)

From Fan–Johnstone–Sun “Spiked Covariances…” (the attached `Spiked Covariances.pdf` / your One-Pager summary):

* Data follow a **multivariate random / mixed effects model** with independent random effects and errors (example in their intro is a balanced one-way model). See `Spiked Covariances.pdf` text near Eq (1.1).
* High-dimensional regime: number of traits (p) and sample size (n) grow together with (p/n) bounded away from 0 and ∞, and design dimensions scale similarly. See `Spiked Covariances.pdf` **Assumption 2.1(a–c)**.
* Each variance component covariance (\Sigma_r) is “**spherical + spikes**”:
  (\Sigma_r=\sigma_r^2 I + V_r\Theta_r V_r^\top), low-rank spike part, bounded spike magnitudes. See `Spiked Covariances.pdf` **Assumption 2.1(d)**.
* Algorithm 1 logic: search combinations (B(a)=\sum_r a_r B_r); for outlier (\hat\lambda) of (\hat\Sigma(a)) with (t_s(\hat\lambda,a)=0) for (s\neq r), estimate target spike (\hat\mu=\hat\lambda/t_r(\hat\lambda,a)). See `Spiked Covariances.pdf` **Algorithm 1**.

### What the repo implements (and where it approximates)

* The repo implements the **Algorithm 1 idea** in a finite-sample, operational form:

  * candidate generation / search (`fjs.dealias_search`),
  * MP edge and (t)-vector computation (`fjs.mp`, `fjs.theta_solver`, `fjs.robust_edge`),
  * a **gating layer** with thresholds and guardrails (`fjs.gating`),
  * an **overlay** application that modifies a baseline covariance (`fjs.overlay`).
    Source: `project_state/MODULE_SUMMARIES.md#srcfjs`, `project_state/FUNCTION_INDEX.md`.

**Approximation #1 (search over (a))**
Theory wants search over all (a\in S^{k-1}); implementation uses a grid/solver + heuristics. That’s fine, but then **you need diagnostics** showing the search actually finds the relevant roots and doesn’t hallucinate them.

**Approximation #2 (exact “(t_s=0)” becomes thresholding)**
Implementation cannot enforce equality; it uses tolerances plus guardrails (isolation, stability, etc.). This is where calibration matters. The project flags this explicitly. Source: `project_state/RESEARCH_NOTES.md#Conceptual mismatch points`, `project_state/OPEN_QUESTIONS.md` (thresholding & calibration questions).

### Where the assumptions are violated in your finance application

* **Independence / exchangeability is violated**: returns are time-series with volatility clustering and cross-asset dependence. The Markowitz high-dim paper even emphasizes how lack of robustness shows up under time-correlation/heavy tails; it still finds risk-underestimation phenomena, but distributional assumptions matter. (Attached `effectsinmarkowitzproblem.pdf`, abstract/intro.)
* **Design model mismatch**: calendar groupings (DoW, vol-state, week-of-year) are not literal random effects; they are *labels derived from the same time series*. That creates selection and dependence between “design” and “noise.” The repo already calls out that mapping is the central conceptual risk. Source: `project_state/RESEARCH_NOTES.md#Conceptual mismatch points`.
* **Spiked + spherical noise**: spiked structure is plausible (factor-like covariance), but spherical idiosyncratic noise is not; you try to mitigate with factor prewhitening + robust scatter. Source: `project_state/PIPELINE_FLOW.md#Prewhitening (optional)`, `project_state/MODULE_SUMMARIES.md#experiments` (`experiments/prewhiten.py`).
* **Finite-sample calibration transfer**: thresholds calibrated on Gaussian synthetic may not transfer to elliptical/heavy-tailed/time-dependent data. Your nested null-kill-test suggests calibration/gating is currently not even internally consistent. Source: `project_state/CURRENT_RESULTS.md#Nested synthetic kill-test (20251217)`.

### Bottom line on alignment

* **One-way (k=2) looks closest** to the theory if you enforce balancing and work on factor-residuals.
* **Nested (k=3) is currently not credible**: you have (a) real-data detection drought AND (b) synthetic null acceptance ~100% under some settings. That combination screams “implementation/gating bug or miswired calibration,” not “subtle empirical effect.” Source: `project_state/CURRENT_RESULTS.md`.

## Gaps, risks, and possible pivots

### Gaps / risks (high priority)

* **Validity gap (nested)**: FPR≈1 under null kills credibility. Until fixed, nested must be treated as “broken prototype,” not research evidence. Source: `project_state/CURRENT_RESULTS.md#Nested synthetic kill-test (20251217)`.
* **Evaluation contamination**: the `variance_forecast_from_components` “cap” makes interpretation of ΔMSE and “percent_changed” ambiguous unless you (a) toggle it, (b) report with/without, and (c) justify it. Source: `project_state/RESEARCH_NOTES.md` (cap), `project_state/TEST_COVERAGE.md` (no tests).
* **Design mismatch**: calendar groupings are not random effects; the paper’s (U_r) and B-matrices are not “free choices” in time-series. If you don’t address this explicitly, reviewers will. Source: `project_state/RESEARCH_NOTES.md#Conceptual mismatch points`.
* **Detection drought vs harm tension**:

  * If overlay almost never triggers (weekly), it can’t matter.
  * If it triggers but harms (daily), you need stronger gating or a different way to incorporate de-aliased spikes.

### Concrete pivots (1–3)

1. **Pivot A (recommended if you want a finance paper fast): drop nested entirely.**
   Focus on **one-way designs only** (weekly one-way + daily DoW/vol) and answer: *when does de-aliasing change risk forecasts / weights, and is it beneficial under any regime?*

   * Pros: closest to theory; fewer moving parts.
   * Cons: might still be neutral/harmful, but at least you can finish cleanly.

2. **Pivot B: reframe to “guardrails + calibration” rather than “performance boost.”**
   Paper contribution becomes: *operationalizing Algorithm 1 under finite samples with robust edges + FPR-controlled gating*, showing (i) aliasing correction on synthetic, (ii) calibrated detection behavior on finance-like elliptical/time-correlated synthetic, (iii) cautious real-data diagnostics.

   * Pros: survivable even with weak real-data gains.
   * Cons: requires serious calibration experiments beyond Gaussian i.i.d.

3. **Pivot C: change how the overlay is applied (partial overlay).**
   Instead of “replace covariance whenever a spike is found,” do **local eigenvalue substitution only**:

   * keep baseline eigenvectors (or factor subspace),
   * adjust only the top-ℓ eigenvalues (or only the component covariance used in weekly reconstruction),
   * enforce a shrink-to-baseline prior on (\hat\mu).
     This reduces the “one bad detection nukes covariance” failure mode (consistent with percent_changed≈1 when triggered).
   * Pros: may turn harms into neutrality/small gains.
   * Cons: you must justify and document the new rule; otherwise it looks like tuning.
